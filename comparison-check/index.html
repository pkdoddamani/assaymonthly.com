What a check finds · November 2026

In November I checked 33 comparison pages from 24 SaaS companies against the competitor's own pricing page. Ten had claims that page contradicts. The most common mistake is a competitor plan that no longer exists.

  • A changelog tool marked a larger rival as having no voting board, no public roadmap and no EU hosting option. The rival's pricing page lists all three. It also gave the rival's starting price as $49 a month, and the rival has a free plan.
  • A website feedback tool said project needs can push teams onto the rival's pricier plans. The rival includes unlimited projects on every plan. The page's own "verified" date was nearly six months old.
  • A social media scheduling tool lists a rival's "Agency" plan at $120. The rival's pricing page today shows three plans, and Agency isn't one of them. The page's own metadata says it was last changed in August 2024. The same retired plans appear on 14 of that company's comparison pages, each in six languages.

In the other 23 I found nothing I could confirm as wrong, so most pages hold up. Names are withheld so the companies can fix their pages first.

Why it matters

Someone reading a comparison page is close to buying, and the competitor's pricing page is one tab away. If they can disprove one line, they discount the rest of the page, including the parts that are true. These pages go stale quietly: nothing breaks when a competitor adds a free plan or ships the feature you said they lack.
